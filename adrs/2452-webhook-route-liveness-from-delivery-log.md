# ADR 2452: Webhook route liveness from GitHub's delivery log, not an authoritative DNS walk

## Status

Accepted. Supersedes ADR 2425 (decisions 1–8: the public-DNS probe, its `pending-dns` state, its 10-minute panel entry, its reconnect call and the startup-replay hold). Builds on ADR 2144 (delivery replay), ADR 2143 and ADR 2142 (the App receivers) and ADR 2351 (the intake dedupe).

## Context

ADR 2425 made the tailscale transport report healthy only once its Funnel name resolved publicly, by walking the DNS delegation from the root hints with recursion off. On a network that redirects all port-53 traffic to a local resolver the walk never gets an authoritative answer. The 0.0.90 gate host sits behind a Firewalla router that does this: the roots, 1.1.1.1, 8.8.8.8, 9.9.9.9 and an unrouted TEST-NET address all answer `REFUSED` with RA set, over IPv4, IPv6 and TCP. Corporate networks, a forced Pi-hole and some ISPs behave the same way. The probe could then only say `funnel: unknown`, and after its grace it raised a warning for a condition that was not a fault. Such networks are tolerated, not worked around.

The probe also answered a narrower question than the one that matters. Whether the name resolves publicly says nothing about whether Tailscale routes the Funnel to the node or whether the receiver acknowledges. GitHub's own resolvers do the lookup that matters and GitHub records every attempt (status code, status text, time) in the App's delivery log (`GET /app/hook/deliveries`), which the delivery replayer already reads.

## Decisions

1. **Remove the DNS probe.** It is deleted together with `pending-dns`, `listening (not publicly resolvable)`, the `tailscale_funnel_dns` panel entry and its 10-minute grace, the one-shot reconnect on resolution, the startup-replay hold (`startTailnetReplay`, `SkipStartupReplayIfPending`), `funnelDNSResolverFactory`, `SetFunnelDNSWaitForTest` and the direct use of `golang.org/x/net/dns/dnsmessage` (the module stays in the build graph through tailscale as `// indirect`). Keeping it as a secondary hint was rejected: to satisfy "a DNS-intercepting network never produces a warning or an unhealthy mode" it would need three behaviour changes (an unknown result raises nothing, a definite pending no longer degrades `webhook.mode`, and the replay hold goes), and what would remain is a second reachability source with its own precedence rules and no information the delivery log lacks. Removal also deletes the only coupling that could keep the startup replay from running because of a DNS path. The startup replay now runs after the fixed 90 s grace (`tailnetReplayStartGrace`) only.
2. **Liveness is its own value, on a new `route` object.** `fabrik_health.webhook.transports[].route` carries `liveness` (`reachable`, `unreachable` or `unknown`), `reason` (GitHub's status text, only when unreachable) and `evaluated_at`. It applies to the tailscale and `direct` transports and is omitted for the others. It does not reuse `funnel`: `funnel` is a tsnet value and does not exist for `direct`.
3. **Passive signal: the delivery log.** The controller (`engine/webhook_liveness.go`) reads the log newest-first (at most 2 pages of 100, 24 h back) and the App's hook URL (`GET /app/hook/config`). The list endpoint has no per-attempt URL, so the decisive attempts are verified with `GET /app/hook/deliveries/{id}` (new `AppHookDeliveryDetail.URL`, `Reconciler.FetchAppHookDelivery`), at most 3 reads per evaluation; an attempt whose URL cannot be read, or that went to another URL, counts for nothing. A 2xx to the current URL within 15 minutes is `reachable`. A run of `livenessFailRun` = 2 consecutive failed attempts to the current URL, the newest within 30 minutes, with no later 2xx, is `unreachable` with the newest attempt's `status` text. The run is 2, not the issue's initial 3: probes are rate-limited to one per 15 minutes, and each failure is GitHub's own record. Failed means a status code outside 200–299 including 0 and 3xx (the replayer's rule). Pings and throttled attempts (`throttled_at` set, or a status mentioning throttling) are neither success nor failure. A failure older than the controller's start plus the warm-up (90 s for tailscale, none for `direct`; counted from when the controller runs, after the node and its Funnel are up, not from its construction, which precedes a possibly slow `Up`) never counts: a new node's Funnel ingress needs about that long to route.
4. **Active signal: a probe redelivery.** When the log decides nothing (start-up, a transport reconnect through the composite handler, a quiet period of 15 minutes, a changed hook URL, or the single early retry 2 minutes after an inconclusive probe) the controller requests `POST /app/hook/deliveries/{id}/attempts` for the newest log delivery that the intake ledger holds, that is not a ping, that was received at most 20 hours ago and that has been probed fewer than twice in 24 hours. The result is the redelivered copy arriving at the receiver or its new attempt in the log, polled every 20 s for 3 minutes. A timeout, or a failed attempt that does not make a run, is `unknown` (plus the one early retry), never `unreachable`. With no eligible delivery nothing is requested and the state stays `unknown` until a real delivery arrives (which itself decides `reachable`). The App webhook has no ping endpoint (the REST page lists config get/patch, deliveries list/get and redelivery only), which is why redelivery is the probe.
5. **A probe copy is never applied, and the dedupe alone guarantees it.** The probe path never calls the sink or `intake.Submit` (`TestWebhookLivenessNeverSubmits`), and the intake's dedupe makes the copy a duplicate. There is deliberately no second "ignore probes" guard in the intake: it would make the non-vacuity test (`TestLivenessProbe_NonVacuity_NeutralisedDedupeApplies`: with `FaultNoDedupe` the stale copy IS applied) unable to fail. Observation happens at the transport instead, which sees the verified request before the sink: `Arrival(guid)` reports whether the request is the copy of an outstanding probe, in which case the transport leaves `last_delivery_at` and the event counts alone (a copy of an already-applied delivery is not a new event and must not make the feed look fresh; the live test compares `last_delivery_at`). `fabrik_health.webhook.last_event_at` (the daemon-wide `note()` in `Submit`) still advances; it is display-only and nothing gates on it (audited). Any other verified arrival is a real delivery from GitHub and decides `reachable` at once.
6. **The ledger age margin is the safety constraint.** `deliveryLedger.Seen` does not prune, so it can report an entry that `Accept` would no longer dedupe (older than 24 h, or evicted by overflow); a redelivered copy of such a delivery would be applied to the board. The probe therefore uses a new read-only `ReceivedAt(id, now)` that judges expiry, and only picks a delivery received within 20 hours: well inside the 24 h window and away from overflow. GitHub keeps the log for 3 days (longer than the ledger), so the ledger, not GitHub, bounds eligibility.
7. **`unreachable` makes the transport unhealthy.** `state()` treats it like the old pending-dns did: `webhook.mode` must not read healthy while GitHub cannot deliver, and the row reads `listening (route unreachable)`. Only GitHub-recorded failures can cause it, so a network that intercepts DNS cannot. `unknown` and `reachable` never degrade health.
8. **Read failures are never `unreachable`.** A failure to read the log, the config or an attempt's URL, or to request a redelivery (401/403/404, a rate limit, a 422, a transport error) leaves the value unchanged. A passive `unreachable` verdict whose deciding failures are older than the last real arrival that decided `reachable` is dropped, because the log read may predate that delivery. A `reachable` value with no fresh evidence for 30 minutes ages out to `unknown`. An `unreachable` value ends when the log stops supporting it: at the first passive read that is undecided because the deciding failure (`failAt`) is older than the 30-minute failure window, and that neither a probe nor a real delivery decided, it drops to `unknown` (`dropStaleUnreachable`, run after `maybeProbe`), and the panel entry resolves; the 24-hour age-out remains only as the backstop when the log cannot be read at all. A changed hook URL drops the old value and re-checks at once. Verdicts are serialised end to end (state write and sink call, `decideMu`), because they are recorded from the HTTP handler and the controller goroutine and the transport row must end on the controller's last value. A probe copy that arrives at a receiver that knows its own URL (tailscale) is not counted while the App points elsewhere.
9. **Warnings panel.** A `webhook_route_unreachable` entry (subject: the transport, detail: GitHub's reason) is raised once an `unreachable` verdict has held for 10 minutes and is resolved when the value is anything else. `unknown` raises nothing.
10. **Volume is bounded (FR-014).** One outstanding probe; a minimum 15 minutes between checks (30 while `unreachable`); at most 4 requests in any rolling hour; a delivery probed at most twice per 24 hours; one early retry per inconclusive episode, which is consumed when it comes due even if nothing can be probed (the delivery's budget or the hourly cap is spent), so an unusable retry cannot make every tick a full evaluation. The constants are variables only so tests can shrink them (`SetLivenessConstantsForTest`); there is no operator configuration.
11. **Coexistence with the replayer (FR-015).** The replayer requests only deliveries the ledger does not hold and that have no 2xx attempt; the probe requests only deliveries the ledger holds. The sets are disjoint, a failed probe attempt of a received delivery is ignored by the replayer (`g.ok`), and neither shares the other's per-delivery budget. `hookDeliveryAPI` stays a two-method interface, so `SetReplayAPIForTest` fakes are unchanged; the detail read is an optional second interface found by type assertion on the same object.

## Consequences

- A host behind DNS interception gets an accurate `reachable`/`unreachable` with no warning, and no DNS code remains.
- The tailscale transport no longer reports `funnel: pending-dns` for a brand-new name. Until the name resolves, GitHub's attempts fail, and after the 90 s warm-up the route reads `unreachable` (after two failures, one of them possibly a probe) and the replay pass runs at 90 s regardless; the redeliveries it requests before the name resolves fail and spend their per-delivery budget (the cost ADR 2425 avoided). The follow-up pass about 3 minutes later and the reconnect passes re-request once each, and the periodic reconcile remains the safety net. This is accepted: the probe's guarantee was only as good as DNS visibility, and the hold made the replay depend on it.
- A quiet bed with no delivery in the ledger within 20 hours stays `unknown` until something arrives.
- Reading `unreachable` can lag a break by one passive interval (5 minutes) plus a probe and its early retry (about 8 minutes), because GitHub sends nothing on its own when there is nothing to send.
- Reading a recovery is bounded too: the log keeps saying `unreachable` for its 30-minute failure window and a quiet bed has no real delivery to say otherwise, so the controller probes while `unreachable` as well (still the 30-minute interval, 4 per hour and 2 per delivery caps). A probe matches only a redelivery attempt that the log did not already show when the probe was requested, so the early retry cannot end on the first probe's attempt. With a single eligible delivery whose per-delivery budget is spent (so no probe is possible), the verdict drops to `unknown` at the first passive read after the failure window passes (decision 8), at most one passive interval later.

## Unverified GitHub behaviour (check on the bed)

- Whether a redelivery targets the hook's current URL or the original one. Current is expected, and it is what makes the broken-route live leg meaningful; a copy that went to the old address is not counted (decision 8).
- The `status_code` GitHub records for connect failures (0 or 502); both are failed, and `status` is carried verbatim.
- Delivery-log lag (minutes), which is why a probe's result is polled for 3 minutes and a miss is `unknown`.
- No redelivery rate limit is documented; the cadence is conservative by construction (decision 10).
